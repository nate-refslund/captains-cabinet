# Your First Week with Cabinet

A short cheat sheet to get the most from your AI executive team. Read once after install; come back when you want fresh ideas.

---

## 3 goals for your first week

Before the feature list: the founders who get the most from Cabinet in week 1 pick **3 concrete things they want to accomplish** — not features to try, but actual work outcomes. Examples:

1. "Get a morning briefing set up so I don't start my day in my inbox."
2. "Have my CRO sweep one competitive question I've been putting off."
3. "Get a draft response to that stuck conversation I've been avoiding."

Pick your 3 and tell your CoS on Day 1. Everything else in this cheat sheet is supporting tactics for those goals.

---

## How to talk to your Cabinet

Open Telegram. Your **CoS** (Chief of Staff) is already there with a Hello message. Reply to it. That's it.

Your CoS is your single point of contact. CoS routes requests to other officers internally — you don't need to remember who does what. Just talk to CoS like you would talk to a thoughtful executive assistant.

A few tone notes:
- Casual is fine. Don't write formal prompts.
- Long messages welcome — your CoS reads context carefully.
- Voice notes work. Your CoS transcribes them.

## 10 things to try this week

### Day 1 — Get the feel

1. **Tell your CoS what your week looks like.** "Hey, here's my week. Anything you'd handle for me?" — CoS surfaces routine items you could delegate.

2. **Ask for a morning briefing.** "Can you brief me each morning at 7 AM on what's worth my attention?" — CoS sets this up; you wake to a structured summary instead of scattered inputs.

3. **Try a quick "second opinion."** Hand CoS a draft email, decision, or memo. "What would you change here?" — Your CPO or CRO weighs in via CoS.

### Day 2 — Delegate a real thing

4. **Forward a stuck conversation.** Paste a Slack/email thread + ask "How would you respond?" — CoS drafts something close to your voice (officers adapt to your tone over time).

5. **Run a 15-min research question past your CRO.** "What's the market doing in [your space] this month?" — CRO sweeps + comes back with a brief. This is what your CRO does well — give it a topic.

6. **Have your CPO audit something user-facing.** A signup page, an email, an onboarding flow. "What's broken or confusing here?" — They walk through it with fresh eyes.

### Day 3-5 — Build a rhythm

7. **Let your CTO look at a real engineering question.** "Quick sanity check — should we use X or Y for [this problem]?" — Build/buy framing, tradeoffs, what they'd pick.

8. **Use your COO for a process question.** "What's the simplest way to handle [recurring annoying task]?" — They reach for operational simplicity.

9. **Notice the audit log.** Open your Cabinet dashboard at refslund.ai/dashboard → today's spend, what officers did. This is your transparency layer. You see every action.

### Day 7 — Reflect

10. **Tell your CoS what's working and what's not.** "Week-1 reflection: more of X, less of Y." — CoS adapts the whole Cabinet to your preferences. The feedback shapes how officers respond next week.

## What to expect this week

- **Replies in seconds, not minutes** — your officers are always available.
- **Officers improve with use** — they learn your tone, your priorities, what you delegate and what you handle yourself.
- **You'll feel weird about delegating at first.** Most founders do. By week 3 it stops feeling weird.
- **Voice messages are great for "stuck thinking out loud" moments.** Walk-and-talk; CoS transcribes + responds.

---

## When you need a human

- **Bug or something broken:** Reply to your CoS — they file the ticket internally.
- **Billing question:** Open Stripe portal from your dashboard.
- **Need Nate directly:** Just say so to your CoS — they route you up.

---

## What your Cabinet WON'T do

- Make irreversible decisions without your sign-off (financial, hiring, deletions).
- Use your data to train AI models (Anthropic terms forbid; Cabinet doesn't store your conversations beyond audit log).
- Operate without you knowing — every action is logged, you can audit anytime.

---

## You're paying for an executive team, not a chatbot

The difference: chatbots respond to prompts. Your Cabinet has opinions, remembers context, coordinates between officers, surfaces things you didn't ask about.

Talk to your CoS like you would a smart human colleague. Push back. Argue. Ask for more options. They handle it.
